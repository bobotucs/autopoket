# AutoPocket v2

A second pocketing engine that sits **next to** v1. Nothing in `autopocket/` was changed:
v2 imports v1's plate detection, CAD cutting and pocket rules and replaces only the
planning. Both apps can run at once so you can compare them on the same plate.

| | v1 | v2 |
|---|---|---|
| Web app | `start.bat` / `python run.py` → port 8765 | `python run_v2.py` → port 8766 |
| Command line | `python cli.py plate.step` | `python cli_v2.py plate.step` |
| Code | `autopocket/` | `autopocket2/` (+ v1's modules) |

Pocket rules are identical: 8 mm ribs, 4 mm offset (pockets 8 mm from edges and holes),
2 mm tool radius, through pockets.

## What changed, and which idea from the research it comes from

The research report (on automated branching cutouts in perforated parts) is about a
different feature, but its engineering method carries over:

1. **Sequential filtering, hard constraints never traded for weight.**
   *Report: generate → cheap geometry screen → CAD/DFM validation → FEA → rank only the
   feasible candidates.* `autopocket2/validate.py` measures every rule on the finished
   geometry (edge/hole clearance, web between pockets, tool radius, pocket width/area,
   one connected part, every hole held from several sides). A design that breaks one is
   rejected before any FEA, and the reasons are counted in the report. v1 builds to the
   rules but never measures the result, and treats hole support as a statistic.

2. **Load-path guidance from the blank plate's stress field.**
   *Report: "solve the unmodified component and bias the pattern away from critical load
   paths".* v2 analyses the blank plate, takes the strain-energy field and sets a local
   truss spacing from it: tighter triangles where the plate works hard, bigger pockets
   where it does not (`layout.SpacingField`). The field is mirrored onto itself so
   symmetric plates stay symmetric.

3. **Rib pruning (evolutionary structural optimisation).**
   *Report cites ESO multiple-cutout optimisation.* After a layout is analysed, the
   interior ribs that carry the least strain energy are removed a few at a time (at most
   one per pocket per round, the merged pocket must stay compact, holes must stay held),
   then the design is re-checked and re-analysed (`layout.prune_ribs`).

4. **No arbitrary load assumption.** *Report: don't let one modelling choice decide the
   verdict.* v1 clamps the plate at one end only, so a plate and its mirror image can
   score differently. v2 clamps each end in turn (6 load cases) and uses the worst one.

5. **Candidate archive and ranking.** Uniform truss, load-guided truss and isogrid layouts at every spacing,
   each with up to 3 pruning rounds, all go into one archive. Only feasible designs
   compete, on weight saved, with lower peak stress breaking near-ties. If none meets the
   target, the stiffest is offered and flagged. No rule is ever relaxed to get an answer.

6. **Verification and explicit status.** *Report: preview ≠ CAD-valid ≠ manufacturable ≠
   analysis-pass; mesh-convergence checks.* The chosen design is re-analysed on a ~3×
   finer mesh, the cut solid's volume is checked against the 2D plan, and the report
   carries a status such as `CAD_VALID + DFM_PASS + ANALYSIS_PASS`.

7. **Benchmark + ablation.** *Report: benchmark suite, ablations, every bug a regression
   test.* `bench/compare.py` runs both versions on every sample and re-grades all their
   designs with one independent grader (finer mesh, both clamp ends, same rule checker).
   With guidance and pruning switched off, v2 reproduces v1's layout exactly.

8. **Symmetric grid between the holes (your rule).** The rib grid is mirrored across
   each outline axis the screw holes are symmetric about (at least 75 % of the holes have
   a twin across it, within 3 mm, so holes a hair off true mirror positions still pair
   up). A hole without a twin is *not* mirrored: there is no "phantom" keep-out on the
   other side. That side keeps a normal pocket, and the lone hole only reshapes the
   pockets right next to it. Pairs that are slightly off get their keep-outs evened out
   so their pockets match exactly. When every hole has its twin, symmetry is a hard rule
   (pockets < 2 % off their mirror image). Otherwise it is only reported.
   (`features.Domain`; changed 2026-10-24: the old phantom keep-outs left small,
   pointless cutouts where a full pocket fits.)

8b. **No bent pockets (your rule).** A pocket that bends round a rib (a chevron or
   boomerang) is cut at its bend into two straight pockets (`shapes.bend_split`), rib
   pruning may not create one, and the validator rejects any left: at most 8 % of a
   pocket's convex hull may be rib (a chevron is ~25 %). Wrapping round a hole band or an
   edge clearance doesn't count. Voronoi webs used to lose the rib lying on a symmetry
   axis (floating point), which merged two cells into one chevron; that is fixed.

8c. **No squeezed leftovers (your rule).** A pocket under 30 % of the layout's median size
   that is pressed against a hole band is the odd leftover between a hole and two ribs.
   It is left solid. As a separate design, analysed like a rib-pruning step, v2 also takes
   out the rib between the leftover and a neighbour so the two become one proper pocket
   (`layout.absorb_squeezed`). That version is kept only if it passes every rule and the
   stiffness target, since the rib it removes can be one that carries load. Small cells
   along the outline are kept.

9. **No thin wedge tips (your rule).** Where a rib meets another rib, a hole band or the
   frame at a shallow angle, v1 can leave a long tapering wedge that a 1/8 in (3.175 mm)
   end mill can't follow and that leaves slender, flexible ribs. v2 cuts every pocket
   corner sharper than 45° back to a round end 8 mm wide (`shapes.blunt`), and the
   validator rejects any layout that still has one.

10. **CheeseIt-style isogrid (from the Onshape FeatureScript you shared).** CheeseIt lays
    a regular triangle grid over the face and turns each cell into a pocket: inset by half
    a wall + tool radius, clip to the face inset by a full wall + tool radius, grow back by
    the tool radius. v2 adds that grid as two more layout families (rows along the long
    side, and turned 90°), with a grid node on the plate's symmetry centre so the grid is
    mirror-symmetric both ways. Its cells go through the same pocket cutting, wedge
    blunting, hole-support struts, rib pruning and checks as the truss layouts.

11. **Per-feature protection (your request).** After uploading, click any hole, outline
    side or corner in the viewer (shift-click for several, or use the "All bearing
    bores / All screw holes / All sides / All corners" chips) and set how much solid
    material is kept around it. For a corner that is a radius around the corner point.
    The protected area is shaded live. Rib width, tool radius and the default clearance
    are editable in the rules panel. A hole's clearance is joined with its mirror twin's,
    so twin holes with different values still get matching pockets; a lone hole's
    clearance is never mirrored.

12. **Engineering mode (from the FTC deep research and ChatGPT's report).** Both reports
    agree that the loads and supports matter more than the algorithm, and that treating
    every hole as fixed is wrong. In the same picker, mark holes as **bolted mounts**
    (green) or **loaded bearings** (red). The FEA then holds the plate over a washer-sized
    ring at each mount and loads each bearing bore with a cosine pressure, in 4 in-plane
    directions and out of plane, instead of the generic end clamp. Guidance, rib pruning
    and the stiffness target all use those cases. With a load per bearing in newtons it
    also estimates peak stress, safety factor and deflection, clearly labelled as a
    screening estimate. The report lists every input with its source (user-specified /
    assumed). Without roles the result is labelled "generic screening loads".

13. **Correct stresses (found by the research).** v1's FEA reads stress at each
    element's centre, which in a one-layer plate model is the mid-plane, where bending
    stress is zero: out-of-plane bending stress came out ~32x too low. v2 now reads it at
    the top and bottom faces, uses a solid-shell material law (no Poisson thickness
    locking), and takes its percentile only from fully solid cells away from loads and
    supports. `tests/test_fea2.py` checks it against hand calculations: cantilever
    deflection 0.995 of beam theory, surface bending stress = Mc/I, in-plane deflection
    within the Timoshenko value, and a mirrored plate giving the same stiffness. v1's FEA
    is unchanged.

14. **It learns from every plate (from the self-improvement research report).** The
    learning lives in the tool, not in Claude: Claude doesn't retrain between
    conversations, but `knowledge/autopocket.db` grows with every run, and every new
    plate is planned with what is in it. Every design generated is stored with its
    geometry features, style, FEA result and an evidence tier. Only FEA-checked rows ever
    train anything; a prediction is never fed back as truth. Three things learn:
    - **Within a plate:** after the spacing grid, each layout family's spacing is bisected
      where it crosses the stiffness target, so picks land near the target instead of
      wherever the fixed grid stopped.
    - **Across plates:** families that never won on the 3 most similar past plates are
      searched on a coarser grid, and winners go first. A nearest-neighbour surrogate
      predicts each design's stiffness before FEA, with an uncertainty, and skips FEA only
      when it is confident the design misses the target by a wide margin. It never
      approves anything: the pick still gets full FEA, the finer-mesh check and every
      rule. Its gate opens only with at least 4 plates and 120 checked designs, and only
      if a leave-one-plate-out test shows it would never have skipped a design that met
      the target. Designs unlike anything seen before are always analysed.
    - **Your style:** your hand-pocketed plates in `examples/` are measured (pocket
      density and size, size uniformity, roundness, corners per pocket, web and edge
      widths). Designs within 1 point of the lightest that pass everything are ranked
      by closeness to that style. Clicking another option or downloading one refits the
      style weights (Bradley–Terry). Style never overrides a rule or the stiffness target.

    `python bench/seed_knowledge.py` loads the style references and pockets the samples;
    `python bench/learning_eval.py [--replan]` tests the learning with each plate held
    out; `python cli_v2.py plate.step` and the web app record every run (`--no-learn` to
    skip).

15. **Pattern choice.** Rules panel → Pattern: **Auto** (tries everything and keeps the
    lightest design that passes), **Isogrid** (regular triangles, both orientations),
    **Freestyle triangles** (truss between your holes, like your hand-pocketed
    examples; plain and load-guided), **Hexagon** (CheeseIt-style honeycomb, both
    orientations) and **Voronoi** (organic web). Voronoi seeds are spaced evenly,
    mirrored, and relaxed with Lloyd's algorithm into an even, near-hexagonal web. The
    web is built in one half or quarter (per the grid's axes) and mirrored, so it is
    exactly symmetric; the guided
    variant uses smaller cells where the plate works hardest. Hexagon and Voronoi webs
    carry load by bending, so per gram they are less stiff than triangles (published
    lattice studies agree), so they also try smaller 16 and 20 mm cells. Every pattern
    goes through the same rules, symmetry, blunt-tip and stiffness checks. Which
    pattern is lightest depends on the plate: isogrid on the side plate (43.5%),
    Voronoi on blank_top (32.4%), all at ≥ 33% stiffness.

16. **Cut animation and simulation view.** After pocketing, **▶ Cut** replays the
    machining: the tool traces each pocket outline in machining order on the original
    plate, and the view ends on the pocketed part. **Simulation** shows the FE model
    itself: every analysis cell coloured by stress (von Mises, at the plate surfaces),
    strain or deflection. You can pick any load case or the worst of all of them, and
    switch between the pocketed and the original plate to compare. With **Show deflection**,
    the nodes move by their computed displacement, exaggerated so it's visible. Values
    are MPa / µε / mm in engineering mode with a load in newtons; otherwise they are
    relative to the original plate. The colour scale tops out at the 99th percentile, so
    the few over-reading cells at hole edges and supports don't wash out the picture.

17. **Tell it how the plate is loaded.** The Loads section takes a plain-English
    description ("chassis side plate, 15 kg robot, weight comes down through the screw
    holes, wheels ride in the bearings"). A built-in rule reader (`autopocket2/loadspec.py`,
    not a chat AI) turns it into which holes hold the plate (green), where the load comes
    in (red), the direction and the force. It shows what it understood and every
    assumption it made, for example half the robot's weight per side plate, or a factor of
    2 for impacts. The direction buttons (down / up / left / right as you see the plate,
    into the plate, or all) are drawn as red arrows. With directions set, each direction
    is one load case with the force shared by all the load holes. The pocket optimiser
    and the simulation both use these load cases.

18. **Sharper simulation.** The Simulation view runs a finer analysis than the planner
    (about 2.7× the cells) and draws it on a ~2 mm triangle mesh of the plate's true
    shape, so hole and pocket edges are real and colours are smooth. It also lists the
    most stressed holes, bearings and ribs (numbered markers on the plate), with MPa and
    safety factor when a load in newtons is known.

The full research report is in `reports/FTC plate lightening engineering.md`, with the
notes behind it in `research_notes/`.

Not taken from the report: lightning/space-colonisation growth (wrong feature for FTC
plates), CGAL, Gmsh/Code_Aster (the built-in plate FEA is enough for relative
comparisons), buckling/fatigue analysis.

The FEA is still a relative estimate with generic load cases. Check critical parts
against your real loads.
